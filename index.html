Radiotherapy for Breast Cancer

Radiotherapy is a standard component of breast-conserving treatment and is also recommended after mastectomy for selected high-risk cases.

Common Roles:
  • Adjuvant therapy (after surgery to prevent recurrence)
  • Breast-conserving surgery follow-up (lumpectomy)
  • Post-mastectomy radiation for advanced stages
Key Reports Needed:
  • Post-surgery histopathology report
  • ER, PR, Her2Neu receptor status
  • Biopsy and staging scans (CT/PET-CT)

Radiotherapy for Head & Neck Cancers

Radiotherapy is a primary treatment modality for many head and neck tumors, either alone or combined with chemotherapy, to preserve speech and swallowing functions.

Common Roles:
  • Definitive treatment (primary cure)
  • Concurrent Chemoradiotherapy (CCRT)
  • Post-operative radiotherapy
Key Reports Needed:
  • Biopsy/Histopathology report
  • Contrast MRI or Contrast CT of face/neck
  • Whole body staging (PET-CT)

Radiotherapy for Lung Cancers

Radiotherapy plays a vital role in both Small Cell and Non-Small Cell lung cancers, ranging from curative intent to managing symptoms like airway blockage or pain.

Common Roles:
  • Definitive Chemoradition
  • Sequential Radiotherapy
  • Stereotactic Body Radiotherapy (SBRT) for early stages
  • Prophylactic Cranial Irradiation (PCI)
Key Reports Needed:
  • Bronchoscopy / biopsy report
  • PET-CT Scan
  • Pulmonary Function Tests (PFT)

Radiotherapy for Brain Tumors

Radiation is used to treat both primary brain tumors (like Gliomas) and metastatic brain lesions, focusing on tumor control while protecting critical brain functions.

Common Roles:
  • Post-operative radiation (following resection)
  • Stereotactic Radiosurgery (SRS) for small lesions
  • Whole Brain Radiotherapy (WBRT)
Key Reports Needed:
  • Brain MRI (Pre & Post-Surgery)
  • Surgical histopathology & IHC molecular markers
  • Post-op discharge summary

Radiotherapy for Prostate & Urinary Cancers

Highly targeted radiation serves as an effective curative alternative to surgical removal for localized prostate cancer and bladder preservation plans.

Common Roles:
  • Definitive External Beam Radiotherapy (EBRT)
  • Androgen Deprivation Therapy (ADT) combination
  • Salvage radiotherapy after prostatectomy
Key Reports Needed:
  • Serum PSA levels (serial tracking)
  • Prostate biopsy & Gleason Score
  • MRI pelvis / PSMA PET-CT

Palliative Radiotherapy

Short-course radiation therapy is extremely effective at relieving symptoms, reducing bone pain, controlling bleeding, and improving quality of life for advanced cancer cases.

Common Roles:
  • Pain relief for painful bone metastases
  • Decompression for spinal cord compression
  • Control of bleeding or respiratory symptoms
Key Reports Needed:
  • Primary histopathology
  • Imaging of the symptomatic site (X-Ray/MRI/CT)
  • Current medications & pain control log

Patient questions

Simple answers before you visit.

Do I need an appointment?

Both appointment and walk-in visits are accepted. Calling ahead is recommended for smoother coordination.

What reports should I bring?

Bring biopsy reports, imaging films or CDs, PET/CT or MRI reports, previous treatment summaries and current medicines.

Is radiotherapy suitable for every cancer case?

No. Suitability depends on cancer type, stage, patient condition and treatment plan after specialist evaluation.

Can I request a second opinion?

Yes. A second opinion can help patients and families understand treatment options before deciding next steps.
